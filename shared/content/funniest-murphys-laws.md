# Funniest Murphy's Laws

The funniest Murphy's Laws are funny because they are true. They turn a familiar failure into a sentence people want to repeat.

## What makes one funny

- It is specific enough to picture immediately.
- It has a clean reversal or escalation.
- It sounds like a joke but behaves like a warning.

## Examples of the shape

Work expands to fill the time available, and then asks for an extension. The printer works until the meeting starts. The line you leave begins moving the moment you leave it.

## The everyday favorites

Some of the funniest laws are about small, universal moments.

- **The other lane.** The lane you did not pick always moves faster, until you switch into it.
- **The missing item.** The thing you are looking for is in the last place you check, because you stop looking once you find it.
- **The demo effect.** A feature that has worked all week fails the moment someone important watches.
- **The toast.** Dropped toast lands butter-side down. There is even a [Buttered Toast Calculator](/calculator/buttered-toast) that explores why.

## Why we laugh at failure

Humor researchers describe a mechanism called benign violation: something is wrong, but safe enough to laugh at. Murphy's Laws fit it well. The mishap is real and recognizable, but it is happening to someone else's toast, or to you in the past tense.

There is also relief in the shared experience. Reading a law that describes your Monday exactly makes the failure feel ordinary rather than personal. The [psychology behind the feeling](/why-murphys-law-feels-true) covers why we notice these failures more than the times things go right.
